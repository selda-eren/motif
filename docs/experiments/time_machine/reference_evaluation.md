# The Time Machine: independent reference for evaluating MORe

Written **before any model output for this book was inspected**, from the evaluator's own knowledge of the novella
(not from MORe, not from any prompt given to MORe). It is NOT used in MORe's processing: the pipeline, prompts and
data are unchanged and never see this file. Its SHA-256 is recorded in `reference_evaluation.sha256` at the time of writing.
Chapter numbers follow the Project Gutenberg edition (I–XVI and Epilogue).

## A. Important developments (what a careful reader would say happens and changes)

1. **Frame and premise (I–III).** The Time Traveller argues, to sceptical dinner guests, that time is a fourth dimension one can
   travel along; he demonstrates a small model machine (II); a week later he returns dishevelled and tells his story (III).
2. **Arrival in 802,701 (IV–V).** He meets the Eloi: small, frail, childlike, fed on fruit, living in ruined communal halls amid
   gardens. His first theory: a Golden Age of ease and communism, humanity having conquered nature and need.
3. **The first theory decays (VI).** He reasons that security and comfort removed the pressures that keep intellect and energy
   alive ("intelligence is the antithesis of change"); the Eloi are not a triumph but a degeneration.
4. **The Morlocks and the stolen machine (VII).** The machine disappears (dragged into the White Sphinx); wells and ventilating
   shafts lead him to the subterranean Morlocks.
5. **Second theory: class division (VIII).** He reads the world as the extreme outcome of the divide between Capitalist and
   Labourer: Eloi as the idle rich, Morlocks as the workers confined underground. He then reverses the master/servant picture:
   the Morlocks now feed, clothe and farm the Eloi like cattle.
6. **Fear and light (IX–XII).** Darkness, fire and matches become decisive: he saves Weena, burns the forest (and Weena is lost),
   finds the decayed Palace of Green Porcelain and takes an iron lever and camphor, and fights through the dark.
7. **The trap (XIII).** The open sphinx and the returned machine turn out to be bait; he escapes with the machine.
8. **The further vision (XIV).** Far beyond, he sees a dying earth, a huge red sun, crab-like creatures and final cold:
   the same pattern of decline carried to cosmic scale.
9. **Return and aftermath (XV–XVI, Epilogue).** He returns, tells the tale, is not believed, goes away again and does not come
   back. The narrator keeps two strange white flowers (from Weena) as evidence of gratitude and tenderness; he refuses the
   Time Traveller's cheerless view of the future.

## B. Interpretive questions a strong reading should answer (and where the answer is spread across the text)

Q1. **Successive theories.** How does the Time Traveller's understanding of the Eloi/Morlock world change (Golden Age →
    degeneration through comfort → class division → inversion of master and servant → cosmic decline), and which earlier
    observations are reread because of later ones (Eloi frailty and leisure, the wells, the sphinx, the cleanliness and gardens)?
Q2. **Frame versus ending.** How does the Time Traveller's early pessimism about "the Advancement of Mankind" (I) connect to
    what he finds, and to the narrator's choice of hope in the Epilogue?
Q3. **The sphinx.** How does the White Sphinx change significance (arrival landmark → hiding place of the machine → bait of the trap)?
Q4. **The machine.** How does the Time Machine change role (a model on a table → a vehicle → something taken → bait → the means of escape)?
Q5. **Fire, light and darkness.** How do matches, camphor, the forest fire and the Morlocks' fear of light link the middle chapters?
Q6. **Weena and the flowers.** How does Weena's care (V) and the flowers she gives resonate with the narrator's flowers in the
    Epilogue; what quality does the book suggest survives decline?
Q7. **Social allegory.** How is the Victorian divide between classes extrapolated, and what is the Time Traveller's moral reading of it?
Q8. **Repeated pattern.** Is there a pattern linking the loss of strength in the Eloi, the loss of vision in the Morlocks and the dying
    world of XIV (adaptation to ease or to darkness, and what that costs)?
Q9. **Ambiguity of the ending.** Why is the Time Traveller's reliability left open, and what does the Epilogue do with that?

## C. What would count as unsupported or misleading

- A unit or meaning whose quotation does not support the statement made about it.
- Attributing to the text a theory the Time Traveller never states, or presenting a mere theory as established fact
  (he repeatedly revises his own explanations).
- Treating the narrator's voice and the Time Traveller's voice as one.
- Interpretation presented as direct statement of the text.
